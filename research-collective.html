A collaboration space in development

Question freely.
Investigate together.

The Evidence Atlas Research Collective is being designed for independent scientists, historians, journalists, investigators and people whose findings have been challenged or overlooked. All contributors are welcome to ask difficult questions; every claim remains open to scrutiny.

Open inquiry, not automatic endorsement. Being disputed or discredited does not by itself prove a researcher right or wrong. Research should be evaluated using methods, reproducible evidence, provenance and constructive criticism. No profiles, submissions or collaborations are publicly active on this page yet.

What we're building

01 · Discover

Researcher directory

Opt-in profiles with fields of expertise, publications, disclosed affiliations and the option to use a public pseudonym. No invented members or implied endorsements.

02 · Collaborate

Investigation workspaces

Private project invitations, role-based access, source libraries, evidence maps, version history and clearly marked unpublished drafts.

03 · Debate

Challenge a finding

Link a specific claim to supporting evidence, conflicting evidence, competing hypotheses and documented responses. Keep disagreements traceable and civil.

04 · Protect

Contributor safety

Consent-first identity disclosure, moderation, redaction and confidential intake planning. No promise of anonymity or secure whistleblower submission before independent security review.

How an investigation should work

1. Propose a research question

Define what is being investigated, what is known, and what remains uncertain.

2. Attach traceable records

Identify original sources, dates, methods, licensing and limitations. Separate primary documents from commentary.

3. Invite competing explanations

Record who advances each interpretation, the strongest supporting and contradictory evidence, and what new finding could change the conclusion.

4. Review before publication

Apply fact-checking, privacy, legal, safety and claim-level publication gates. Clearly label disputed and unresolved material.
