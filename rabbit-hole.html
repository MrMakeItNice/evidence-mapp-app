🐇 The Rabbit Hole

A space for unconventional hypotheses, conspiracy theories, curious questions, and serious scrutiny of powerful institutions. Bring your curiosity. Bring your sources. Be willing to change your mind.

Freedom to question. Responsibility to distinguish evidence.

Make It Nice supports open expression and good-faith disagreement. Its central objective is to organize documented facts, compare competing explanations, and hold power accountable. The historical record gives us good reason to ask hard questions about whether authorities always act in the public interest. That does not make every suspicion true.

Suspicion is not proof. Correlation is not causation. An official explanation can be challenged without assuming an alternative explanation is correct.

No person is beneath respect. No institution is above scrutiny. No theory is above testing.

How to evaluate a proposed connection (review guide, not live graph labels)

These are proposed evidence classifications for a future reviewed relationship graph. Existing citation edges are research references, not automatically verified connections.

Documented relationship
A specific source directly supports the stated connection. Example: the CIA retrospective documents BLUEBIRD's renaming to ARTICHOKE; it does not establish every alleged operation.
Thematic similarity
Two subjects share a topic or method, but similarity alone does not establish coordination or causation.
Investigator hypothesis
A testable but unverified proposed connection, with attribution, supporting sources, alternatives and counterevidence still to assess.
Contradicted link
A particular claimed connection is challenged by identified contrary evidence. Missing evidence alone is not proof that a link is false.

Review gate: Do not present a citation as a documented relationship, or publish a hypothesis, without checking the original record and its limitations.

Project BLUEBIRD is not Project Blue Beam

The names sound similar, but Evidence Atlas must not treat them as aliases or infer an operational relationship from their wording.

DOCUMENTED · CIA PROGRAM

Project BLUEBIRD

A CIA memorandum dated April 5, 1950 describes an interrogation project involving drugs, hypnosis and polygraph techniques. The canonical investigation is Case #2 (record ID 3). Case #31 (record ID 32) repeats the title as an index cross-reference, not a separate historical program.

UNVERIFIED · RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Project Blue Beam

A theory associated with Canadian writer Serge Monast in the 1990s. A December 17, 2024 Guardian report describes the theory's history; this is secondary reporting about a claim, not evidence that the alleged operation existed. No authenticated official record establishing a government operation called Project Blue Beam has been identified in the reviewed sources. It is not an alias for BLUEBIRD or an established Case File.

How a theory becomes a documented case

1. Question → 2. Testable claim → 3. Primary sources → 4. Independent corroboration → 5. Counterevidence → 6. Reviewed finding.

Every claim should carry a clear label: Speculation, Unverified, Disputed, Supported, or Established. Only reviewed, sourced material belongs in the factual case archive. A label can change when stronger evidence appears.
