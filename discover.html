Our editorial commitment

Honesty before allegiance.

We prioritize original reporting from identifiable journalists who show their evidence, explain their methods, disclose conflicts, and correct mistakes. The same standard applies whether a story challenges our views or agrees with them.

What earns attention

  • A named reporter, publication date, and clear reporting versus opinion labels.
  • Documents, data, on-the-record sources, and meaningful independent corroboration.
  • Disclosed ownership, funding, relevant conflicts, and a fair opportunity for subjects to respond.
  • Visible corrections, follow-up reporting, and honest acknowledgment of uncertainty.

Accountability watch / Land ownership & food systems

Bill Gates, farmland & the public interest.

Start with what can be checked

The Land Report identifies Gates as a major private U.S. farmland owner. Its profile distinguishes farmland from total landholdings; acreage figures need a date, category, and source. This starting note is not a complete ownership audit.

Read The Land Report’s ownership profile ↗

His stated explanation

In his January 2023 AMA, Gates described farmland as a productive investment managed by a professional team. That is his stated rationale, which should be attributed and examined alongside independent reporting and records.

Read Gates’s own account / January 2023 ↗

Questions for the record

  • Which companies hold agricultural assets, and what dated records substantiate ownership?
  • How have purchases, leases, land use, and farmer access changed?
  • What do independent reporters and affected farming communities document?
  • What financial and policy connections are evidenced, with personal investments and foundation grants kept distinct?

These are research questions, not findings of wrongdoing. Reviewed 19 October 2026. The public-source watch checks for substantive new records and reporting; it cannot establish private intentions or provide complete coverage.

About the project · README

Small acts. Shared responsibility.

Make It Nice is a daily practice of caring for ourselves, each other, and our shared home. The hub leads with constructive journalism and supports self-care, government accountability, and thoughtful comparison of media narratives.

How to use Evidence Atlas

Your reading guide

  1. Search historical cases and read what the cited records say.
  2. Explore reporting by subject and open the original publisher.
  3. Check dates, corrections, source independence and the original context.
  4. Compare opposing evidence before drawing conclusions.
  5. Use the private researcher workspace if you are the authorized owner.
Research methodology

What our evidence labels mean

  • Documented: a specified fact is supported by an identifiable record or finding.
  • Attributed finding: a named court, commission or institution reached a conclusion; note its date and legal status.
  • Contested / alleged: evidence conflicts, or the proposition has not been established.
  • Unassessed: not yet sufficiently reviewed, which is different from disproven.

The original historical index also uses contextual A–D evidence grades. These are not probabilities or guarantees that every allegation in a case is proven.

Trust, privacy and safety

What is public and what stays private?

This public reader can display approved educational summaries without an account. The researcher workspace uses Supabase sign-in and owner-only database permissions for private drafts and records; public source code is not the private database.

External document links may lead to disturbing subject matter or third-party websites. Read our current privacy notice ↗ for additional details.

A privacy summary is not a guarantee of perfect security. Avoid sharing sensitive information about survivors or witnesses.
