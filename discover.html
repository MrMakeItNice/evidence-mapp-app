About the project · README

Understand the world. Help make it nicer.

Evidence Atlas is an independent educational research project focused on asking better questions, following original records, and keeping uncertainty visible.

Our mission

Connections aren't conclusions.

Our historical case library, newsroom and investigation workspaces help readers explore source-grounded information about government operations, public health, accountability and human rights. Inclusion of a person, institution, accusation or timeline overlap does not imply guilt or proof of causation.

How to use Evidence Atlas

Your reading guide

  1. Search historical cases and read what the cited records say.
  2. Explore reporting by subject and open the original publisher.
  3. Check dates, corrections, source independence and the original context.
  4. Compare opposing evidence before drawing conclusions.
  5. Use the private researcher workspace if you are the authorized owner.
Research methodology

What our evidence labels mean

  • Documented: a specified fact is supported by an identifiable record or finding.
  • Attributed finding: a named court, commission or institution reached a conclusion; note its date and legal status.
  • Contested / alleged: evidence conflicts, or the proposition has not been established.
  • Unassessed: not yet sufficiently reviewed, which is different from disproven.

The original historical index also uses contextual A–D evidence grades. These are not probabilities or guarantees that every allegation in a case is proven.

Feature guide

Explore our research areas

Historical case library: Documented and disputed operations, with original case notes.

Newsroom: Current coverage by external publishers, not independently endorsed headlines.

Independent investigations: The private workspace contains dedicated Epstein Files, COVID-19, and Palestine / Gaza groups, plus a research-review queue and timelines.

Connections: Shared documents or timelines are research leads; additional evidence is required for causal conclusions.

Trust, privacy and safety

What is public and what stays private?

This public reader can display approved educational summaries without an account. The researcher workspace uses Supabase sign-in and owner-only database permissions for private drafts and records; public source code is not the private database.

External document links may lead to disturbing subject matter or third-party websites. Read our current privacy notice ↗ for additional details.

A privacy summary is not a guarantee of perfect security. Avoid sharing sensitive information about survivors or witnesses.

Contact and feedback
