Freedom of belief. Responsibility for harm.

This section welcomes believers, former members, questioning people, families, and people of no religion. Its scope includes clergy, religious leaders, organizations, and systems across traditions. Specific conduct and institutional responses are assessed case by case; membership or religious identity does not establish wrongdoing.

We are starting with one sourced investigation. Other traditions can be added as documented research develops. This is an open research scope, not an existing encyclopedia of world religions or a finding that religions are inherently corrupt.

Survivor voices

Your story belongs to you.

People affected by abuse, coercion, silencing, or institutional failures should have a voice and control over what they share. Support must not depend on proving a public case or agreeing with this project’s views.

Story submissions are not open yet. This page has no story form or upload service. A private intake system and an operating moderation process have not been established. Please do not put personal stories, evidence about abuse, contact details, or names of private individuals into the project’s public GitHub issues.

Requirements before story sharing opens

Choice and privacy. Optional pseudonyms; minimal identifying information; a clear explanation of who can read a submission, where it is stored, and how long it is kept. Separate consent for private review and public publication.

Control. A working correction and withdrawal route, with an honest explanation that third-party copies of already published material may persist. No pressure to share, upload evidence, or identify another person.

Review before publication. Redaction of identifying details, a process to assess supporting records and responses, and clear attribution when an experience has not been independently corroborated. Firsthand testimony is valuable without being presented as a court finding.

Protection and support. An adult submission pathway, a separate safeguarding plan for minors, trained moderation, and links to established support services. Evidence Atlas does not currently operate a confidential reporting or emergency-response service.

A starting record, not an accusation list

Accountability begins with the documents.

Official inquiry record

Australia: Royal Commission, Case Study 29

The Commission held hearings in 2015 examining the experiences of survivors and the Jehovah’s Witness organizations’ responses, policies, and prevention procedures concerning child sexual abuse in Australia.

The official case page provides the findings report, hearing transcripts, exhibits, and submissions—including the Watchtower organization’s submission. Read these together and attribute each claim to the relevant document.

Open the official inquiry record ↗

Source type: government inquiry archive. Findings, witness testimony, and parties’ submissions have different evidentiary roles. A submission is an argument; an inquiry report is not a criminal conviction.

Future case files should identify the institution and jurisdiction, dates, alleged conduct, available evidence, response, status of proceedings, and the remedy or policy change being sought. Accountability may include documented corrections, safeguarding changes, survivor support, and lawful reporting or legal processes.

Different claims need different labels.

Evidence labels used in this section
LabelWhat it means
Documented factA specific event or statement supported by an identified record. Proof that an organization published a belief is not proof that the belief is true.
Attributed findingA named court, commission, or investigator reached a conclusion. Give the decision date, scope, procedural status, and any material challenge or appeal.
Firsthand account / allegationA person reports an experience or accusation. Attribute it accurately, seek corroboration where possible, and state what remains unverified.
Interpretation / disputed claimAn explanation, theological claim, or contested factual assertion. Show the reasoning, counterevidence, and uncertainty.

Apply the same standards to every tradition. Distinguish institutional responsibility from individual guilt, and criticism of beliefs from evidence of harm. Avoid public accusation lists, collective blame, doxxing, and promises of legal outcomes.
